## Section B — Knowledge

*Instructions: choose the single best answer. If you do not know, please select "I don't know" rather than guessing.*

*(Every item includes the option: ☐ I don't know)*

**B1.** The type of radiation used in fluoroscopy is:
a) Ultrasound b) X-rays (ionizing radiation) c) Magnetic fields d) Infrared radiation

**B2.** During fluoroscopy, the main source of scattered radiation to staff in the cath lab is:
a) The X-ray tube housing b) The image detector c) **The patient's body** d) The control room

**B3.** If you double your distance from the radiation source, your radiation exposure becomes approximately:
a) One half b) **One quarter** c) One eighth d) Unchanged

**B4.** The three fundamental principles of radiation protection are:
a) Speed, accuracy, planning b) **Time, distance, shielding** c) Collimation, magnification, angulation d) Dose, rate, energy

**B5.** The unit used to express occupational effective radiation dose is:
a) Becquerel b) Gray c) **Sievert (mSv)** d) Watt

**B6.** ⚠ The ICRP-recommended occupational effective dose limit is:
a) 1 mSv per year b) **20 mSv per year (averaged over 5 years)** c) 100 mSv per year d) There is no limit

**B7.** ⚠ The current ICRP-recommended annual equivalent dose limit for the lens of the eye (occupational) is:
a) 150 mSv b) **20 mSv** c) 500 mSv d) 1 mSv

**B8.** ⚠ After a pregnancy is declared, the recommended dose limit to the fetus for the remainder of the pregnancy is approximately:
a) **1 mSv** b) 20 mSv c) 50 mSv d) No specific limit

**B9.** Radiation-induced cancer is classified as which type of radiation effect?
a) Deterministic (tissue reaction) b) **Stochastic** c) Acute d) Mechanical

**B10.** Radiation-induced cataract is best classified as:
a) **A deterministic effect (tissue reaction)** b) A stochastic effect with no threshold c) Not related to radiation d) An infectious complication

**B11.** Scattered radiation exposure to the operator is generally highest when standing:
a) On the image-detector side of the patient b) **On the X-ray tube side of the patient** c) At the patient's feet d) Position makes no difference

**B12.** Using image magnification during fluoroscopy generally:
a) Decreases patient dose b) **Increases radiation dose** c) Has no effect on dose d) Only affects image brightness

**B13.** Steeply angulated projections (e.g., LAO cranial) generally:
a) Reduce operator exposure b) **Increase operator and patient exposure** c) Have no effect on exposure d) Eliminate scatter

**B14.** Collimating the X-ray beam to the area of interest:
a) Increases scatter b) **Reduces dose and improves image quality** c) Increases patient dose d) Is only cosmetic

**B15.** Cine acquisition (recording), compared with standard fluoroscopy, delivers a radiation dose that is:
a) Lower b) About the same c) **Substantially higher** d) Zero

**B16.** ⚠ Which parameter best reflects the total radiation output delivered during a procedure?
a) Fluoroscopy time alone b) **Dose–area product (DAP/KAP)** c) Number of cine runs alone d) Table height

**B17.** ⚠ The typical effective dose to the patient from a diagnostic coronary angiography is on the order of:
a) 0.02 mSv (like a chest X-ray) b) **~5–7 mSv (equivalent to several hundred chest X-rays)** c) 100 mSv d) 1000 mSv

**B18.** A standard lead apron (0.25–0.5 mm lead equivalent) attenuates approximately what proportion of scattered radiation?
a) About 10% b) About 50% c) **90% or more** d) 100% (complete protection)

**B19.** The purpose of the ceiling-suspended lead shield is to:
a) Protect the patient from the primary beam b) **Reduce scattered radiation reaching the operator's head, eyes, and upper body** c) Improve image quality d) Support the image detector

**B20.** ⚠ Wearing leaded glasses primarily protects against:
a) Skin injury of the hands b) **Radiation-induced cataract of the eye lens** c) Thyroid cancer d) Bone marrow suppression

## Section D — Practice (Self-Reported)

*Instructions: how often do you do each of the following during radiation-based procedures?*
*Scale: 1 = Never, 2 = Rarely, 3 = Sometimes, 4 = Often, 5 = Always*

| # | Behavior |
|---|---|
| D1 | I wear a lead apron during procedures. |
| D2 | I wear a thyroid shield during procedures. |
| D3 | I wear lead glasses during procedures. |
| D4 | I position the ceiling-suspended shield / available shielding between myself and the radiation source. |
| D5 | I use table-side lead drapes when available. |
| D6 | I deliberately position myself to reduce my exposure (e.g., away from the tube side). |
| D7 | I step back or maximize my distance from the source during cine acquisition when my role allows. |
| D8 | I remain aware of where scattered radiation is highest during the procedure. |
| D9 | I actively try to minimize fluoroscopy time (or support the operator in doing so). |
| D10 | I monitor or check the radiation dose indicators (fluoro time, DAP, air kerma) during or after procedures. |
| D11 | I wear my personal dosimeter (TLD badge) as required. |
| D12 | I speak up or take action when I notice unsafe radiation practices in the lab. |

> **Objective practice checklist:** where feasible, items D1–D7 and D11 will also be assessed by direct structured observation by the research team (observer checklist to be derived from these items — see roadmap step 10). Self-reported and observed practice will be analyzed separately.
